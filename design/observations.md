# Observations — direction

Status: direction document. No UI, no production code, nothing committed. SLOP is out of scope.

This supersedes the recommendations of two earlier investigations without replacing them:

- `design/participatory-publishing.md` — **the table**: immediate public placement, clearing into assemblies.
- `design/observation.md` — **questions open to observation**: PROBNAYA asks selected people a question, observations accumulate under it, a finding closes it.

Both remain as the record of how this direction was reached.

---

## 1. Direction reset

**Rejected: questions as the foundation.** `observation.md` turned a publishing idea into a research programme. It required people to be asked before they had anything to give, organised them by standpoint, ran them through observation periods, and closed every set with a finding. Each step was individually defensible, and together they built a volunteer panel. The simulations that justified it measured retention and yield, which are a programme's measures. They are not a publication's measures.

**Rejected: a consequence for every contribution.** Both earlier documents assumed an observation needed something to happen to it: an assembly, an issue, a finding, an investigation. That assumption came from wanting to prove the institution was necessary. Publication by an editor is already an institutional act. An observation is allowed to remain an observation.

**Rejected: immediate self-publication.** The table let people place things in public themselves. PROBNAYA is not a host. Someone sends something, PROBNAYA reads it, and PROBNAYA decides whether it belongs.

**Rejected: a formal definition of the unit.** `observation.md` derived a test — occasion, account, thing; "who is living with which machine, and what did one do to the other?" The findings behind it were real. As a gate, the test would have excluded some of the best material in section 4.

**What replaces them** is the simplest version of the original idea:

```text
SOMEONE SENDS SOMETHING SMALL
→ PROBNAYA READS IT
→ PROBNAYA PUBLISHES IT, OR DOES NOT
→ IT REMAINS THERE
```

## 2. What remains useful from the previous investigations

Kept as editorial knowledge, not as rules.

**From the table**

- No `PROB–` mark on another person's words. The mark identifies what PROBNAYA issues.
- The sender's words are never rewritten and never summarised by a generator.
- It is allowed to be small.
- No profiles, per-person pages, comments, reactions, or counts.
- The Interior has no outward direction. Everything in it is done to the holder. That is still true, and it limits how this form can appear there (section 15).

**From the observation investigation**

- **Context usually lives in the material.** "Under a redundancy email" turns a screenshot into an observation. This is something an editor notices when reading, not a field a sender fills in.
- **Generated material alone is rarely an observation.** Generated material that someone *met* often is. Again an editorial sensibility, not a filter.
- **Most strong material is words.** In fifty earlier examples, seven in ten had nothing to attach. Text is the centre of gravity.
- **Noticeability decays.** What is strange in 2026 is ordinary by 2028. Dates matter, and a slow collection gains value by being kept.
- **Workplace and personal material needs an unsigned option.** The most revealing material is the least publishable under a name.
- **Faces, family members, and internal screens** are recurring publication risks.
- **A sender's own later correction** is often more valuable than the original.
- **Immediate signed publication chills careful people and rewards the fastest.** Editorial selection answers this without any other mechanism.
- **Requiring a relation added a ceremony** before anyone could send anything.
- **"Someone should be writing this down"** is a real sentence people say.

**Rejected from the observation investigation:** questions as containers, standpoints as organising principle, observation periods, findings, observer panels, retention as a goal, required occasion or standpoint fields, and the thematic test.

## 3. The simplest definition

> **Observations is where PROBNAYA publishes small things that other people noticed and sent to it.**

Each published observation belongs to the person who sent it. PROBNAYA's part is to read, to decide, to keep what it publishes, and to state plainly that it did so.

### Minimum editorial states

The editorial pass in section 5 was run with the fullest grammar first and then reduced to what was actually used.

| Seen by | States | Used in the first year |
|---|---|---|
| **The public** | Published. Nothing else is ever visible. | 31 |
| **The sender** | Sent. Published, and told so. Kept, and told so. | — |
| **PROBNAYA** | **Received**, then one of **Publish**, **Keep**, or **Decline**. | 31 published, one of them after being kept · 1 still kept · 20 declined |

One further act is not a state: **Ask**. PROBNAYA writes to the sender when something would be publishable with a change that only the sender may make — removing a photograph, choosing an excerpt, confirming a detail. It was used five times in 52.

**Keep earned its place, narrowly.** It was used twice, both times with the sender's agreement: once for material the sender wanted PROBNAYA to know but not publish, once for material the sender wanted published only after a date. Without Keep, both would have been lost or published harmfully. Keep is *not* a private archive, a tip line, or a store of good material for later use. Material sent "not for publication" that PROBNAYA should not hold was declined (candidate 52).

**Decline deletes.** Declined material is not retained. The sender is not told, unless the sender needs redirecting or asked not to be published.

**Totals: 31 published, 1 kept, 20 declined.** Five were asked about; four of those became publishable.

What the pass shows is character, not criteria:

- **Specific beats clever.** 07 was published and 41 was not, though 41 is funnier.
- **Something happened to someone.** Not always a machine, and not always to the sender.
- **The sender's own last sentence often decides it.** 01, 08, 24, 29, 38.
- **Familiarity is a reason to decline**, even for true things. What was noticeable last year can be a genre this year.
- **PROBNAYA does not edit, so it asks.** Asking was the most labour-intensive act and produced several of the best pieces.
- **The acceptance rate is high** because early senders were told about the form personally. From open senders it will be much lower.

## 7. Editorial character and thematic boundary

### Character

Taken from the decisions, not imposed on them:

- **Specific over clever.** A detail beats a punchline.
- **Encountered over held.** Something happened, or was seen, or was found. Opinions, predictions, and positions are declined.
- **The sender's voice, uncorrected.** Nothing is tidied.
- **Small by nature, not by limit.** Several paragraphs are fine when every one is needed.
- **Unforced.** Pieces made for sharing, dunks, quips already in circulation, and generated artefacts with nothing encountered are declined.
- **Not endorsement.** Publishing a belief is fine when it is framed as one. Publishing a claim that borrows PROBNAYA's credibility is not.
- **Care before publication.** Faces, identifiable colleagues, internal screens, patients, and family members prompt a question to the sender, not a quiet edit.
- **Familiarity decays a piece.** True and common is weaker than true and noticed.

### Field

Not an eligibility rule. The collection above sets the field by example:

- **Obvious territory:** AI and agents at work, ordinary machines, interfaces, generated material that was met, corporate computational culture, the domestic machine.
- **Open edge:** a slime mould (21), a hedgehog (46), cows (05), a bee in a timetable gap (30), a church heating system (11). Each belongs because it is seen with the kind of attention PROBNAYA brings to machines: to process, behaviour, learning, systems, rhythm, and how people or animals arrange themselves around them.
- **Outside:** a sunflower as a metaphor (22), a data centre as a pretty picture (09), a nature fact, a claim about signals (23), anything whose only claim is being interesting.

The useful tension is that **the subject is open and the attention is not**. The editorial question stays: *does this belong in PROBNAYA?* A reader of the first year could answer that about a new piece without being told a rule. That is what a recognisable field means.

## 8. Why someone sends something

Not "how do we motivate people". What actually happens in the candidates above.

- **They already read it, or were told about it.** Every early sender heard about Observations from someone. Every later sender had read it. The collection is its own invitation. Nobody sends something to a publication they cannot picture.
- **They have a small thing with nowhere to go.** 11, 20, and 51 would be wasted as social posts and too slight to write up. "Someone should be writing this down" is the feeling; Observations is somewhere that does.
- **It does not have to be funny, clever, or a take.** 27 and 37 would get nothing anywhere else.
- **An editor with taste might want it.** Being published is a small, real recognition that is not exposure: nobody gains followers. PROBNAYA thought it belonged. That is the whole reward.
- **It can be unsigned.** 07, 28, 29, and 38 exist because they could be.
- **It costs almost nothing.** Two minutes, no account, no expectation.

**Senders do not need to return.** Henrik sent one sentence and never sent anything again. That is complete success. K. sent a poem that was declined and later a sentence that was published. Tomasz sent four and two were published. No mechanism brought anyone back; people who noticed something again simply sent it.

## 10. Authorship and attribution

### What the year used

| Form | Pieces | Where it served |
|---|---|---|
| **Full name** | 12 | Work and craft from people comfortable being known: 01, 05, 11, 17, 20, 36. |
| **Initial and surname** | 8 | A middle distance: 06, 10, 24, 30, 33, 37, 40, 46. |
| **Initials only** | 4 | Personal or domestic: 12, 14, 19, 42. |
| **Unsigned** | 7 | Workplaces, grief, distress: 07, 08, 28, 29, 35, 38, 43. |
| **A short context line**, optional | 18 | Where it helps a reader: `dairy farmer, Jutland`, `warehouse, nights`. |

Full names and chosen names cannot be told apart by a reader or by PROBNAYA, so they are not different options. The sender writes the name they want. PROBNAYA does not verify identity. It does not publish a name it has reason to believe is not the sender's.

### The model

- **The sender chooses how they appear**: a name, initials, or unsigned, and optionally a short line of context in their own words. Nothing is required but the choice.
- **No link from an attribution to anything.** Names are text. There is no page per sender and no way to list one person's observations.
- **Unsigned is still clearly someone else's.** `Unsigned` is printed where a name would be. The reader never mistakes it for PROBNAYA.
- **PROBNAYA's own marks are dates and plain provenance only**: `PUBLISHED`, `ADDED BY THE SENDER`, `TEN OF 912 LINES, CHOSEN BY THE SENDER`. Set in the site's mono caps. Never commentary, never a title, never a `PROB–` identifier.
- **One date per piece: publication.** The sender's own time lives in their text when it matters ("in February", "Bus stop, Uppsala, 13:20").
- **A page-level statement** makes the relation explicit once: the words belong to the people named; PROBNAYA published them. This echoes Laboratory Statement 001, which names its drafting models and then states `DECIDED BY PROBNAYA`: making and deciding are different acts, and both are shown.
- **Attached material keeps its own authorship.** A screenshot of generated text is the generator's output, brought by the sender. The sender's line is theirs.

**Attribution changes what people send.** None of the seven unsigned pieces could plausibly have been sent under a name. The option to be unsigned is not a convenience; it is the reason a quarter of the collection exists.

## 11. Relationship to PROBNAYA's own work

**The default lifecycle is complete as it stands.** Someone sends something; PROBNAYA publishes it; it remains there. Nothing below is required.

| Form | Relation | Where PROBNAYA's authorship begins |
|---|---|---|
| **Remains published** | The default. Almost every observation simply stays. | PROBNAYA authored nothing but the decision and the date. |
| **Cited by a PROBNAYA Note** | A Note may quote an observation verbatim, with its attribution as published, and point to it. | At the Note's own sentences. A paraphrase is PROBNAYA's and must not be presented as the sender's words. |

| **Kept material** | Used for nothing without the sender's explicit agreement, and attributed as the sender then chooses. | Same as a citation, and only with consent. |

| **Intake** | Different act: intake asks PROBNAYA for work. A problem sent as an observation is pointed to intake (34). | — |

**Withdrawal overrides citation.** A sender may withdraw a published observation at any time. It is removed completely: no gap, no marker, nothing in its place. ## 13. Recommended public form

**A slow published sequence**, called plainly **Observations**.

Candidate structures compared against the year:

| Structure | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Table** | The act is placing; the reader's experience is reading. See section 12. |
| **Register** | PROBNAYA's register is its own records with states. Others' words should not carry states or look like records. |
| **Pages or editions** | Impose a cadence and a composition the material did not need. |
| **Themed collection** | Taxonomy. The threads in section 9 are better found than labelled. |
| **Archive** | Implies something closed. This is open. |
| **Loose sequence** | **What is actually happening.** Things are published one at a time, in PROBNAYA's order, and stay. |

### Structure

- **One page.** Every observation is fully visible on it. No cards, no teasers, no click-through to read.
- **In publication order, earliest first.** A reader meets the collection in the order it was made, which is how its value accumulates. The most recent piece is reachable from the top by one plain link. Newest-first would privilege recency, which is a feed's value and not this collection's.
- **Each observation has a stable address**, so it can be pointed to and cited. The address shows nothing but that observation.
- **No numbers, no counts, no tags, no search, no filters.** At about thirty a year, the page stays readable for several years. When it does not, split by year.
- **One statement at the head**, in PROBNAYA's voice: what this is, that the words belong to the people named, and that PROBNAYA published them.
- **One quiet way to send something**, stating that publication is not guaranteed.
- **A figure, if any, is not derived from contributions.** The site's live figures are welcome on this page as on any other, but not a visualisation of activity.

### How it stays a publication as it grows

- **Pace is set by the editor,** not by senders.
- **Nothing on the page is about people**: no per-person anything, no counts of senders or pieces.
- **Nothing refreshes.** A returning reader sees what was added since, if anything.
- **Decline stays the ordinary outcome** once the page is public and open senders arrive.

### Scale

- **Five senders.** A handful of pieces a month. PROBNAYA reads, asks, and publishes by hand. The page stays private until it holds about a dozen.
- **Fifty senders.** Perhaps a hundred things a year, of which thirty to forty are published. Asking becomes the main cost and must be used sparingly.
- **Hundreds.** Most things are declined. Asking stops except for exceptional pieces; unpublishable-as-sent becomes decline. The collection's pace does not rise with volume. If spam or volume makes reading impossible, the answer is to narrow who can send, not to publish faster.

## 14. Minimum contribution interaction

Described only to make the editorial model concrete. No layout, no architecture.

**What a reader encounters.** The statement, then the sequence. At the end, or at the head, a single line: send an observation.

**What someone who wants to contribute does.** Sends the thing, from the Observations page. No account and no relation.

**What they provide:**

- **the material**: text, and optionally one or a few small attachments;
- **how they want to appear**: a name, initials, or unsigned, and optionally a short line of context;
- **a way to be reached**, which is never published;
- **two confirmations**: that they may share any attached material, and that it does not show anyone who could be identified without their agreement.

Nothing else. No title, no category, no location or date field, no description of what it is.

**What they read before sending.** Plainly:

```text
PROBNAYA READS EVERYTHING SENT.
PUBLICATION IS NOT GUARANTEED.
NOTHING IS EDITED. IF SOMETHING NEEDS CHANGING, WE ASK YOU.
IF IT IS PUBLISHED, YOU WILL BE TOLD.
IF IT IS NOT, YOU WILL NOT HEAR FURTHER, AND IT IS DELETED.
```

**After sending.** A receipt in the manner of intake: received, and nothing further unless it is published. No status to check.

**If PROBNAYA asks.** One message from PROBNAYA to the sender, naming the one change needed. The sender changes it, or does not.

**If it is published.** One message telling the sender where it is.

**Withdrawal.** At any time, by asking PROBNAYA. It is removed completely. A sender may also add a dated line beneath their own published observation (24).

**How text is treated.** Published as sent, including its spelling and line breaks. PROBNAYA adds no title and corrects nothing.

**How attached material is treated.** Shown with the text, uncropped and unretouched. Location data embedded in photographs is not published. A file too large to read as an observation is either declined or, if the idea is good, the sender is asked to choose a small part (33). Generated material appears as what it is: something the sender met, shown inside their observation, not labelled by PROBNAYA.

**What there is not.** No preview of how it will look, no editing after sending, no tracking, no reply thread.

---

## 15. Minimal relationship with the Interior

The public form comes first, and needs nothing from the Interior. A relation is not required to send.

If a sender holds a relation and chooses to connect an observation to it, the only natural traces are:

- **HISTORY** — `OBSERVATION PUBLISHED`, dated, pointing to it. This is an event that happened within the relation and is no longer current.
- **RELATION / STANDING** — possibly, later, a count of observations published, beside correspondence, held, and investigations.

Not natural, and not proposed:

- **CURRENT** — something sent and not yet read would make CURRENT a submission tracker.
- **HELD** — observations are not issued objects.
- **Any view of one's own submissions** — a creator area.

An unsigned observation may be connected privately; the relation shows it only to the holder. This remains the only outward trace in an Interior built for receiving. The limitation noted in the first investigation stands: the Interior has no place for what a holder gives, and this form does not need one. Nothing here is proposed for the first version.

## 16. Failure modes

| Failure | What it looks like | What prevents or answers it |
|---|---|---|
| **Empty at opening** | Five pleasant pieces and no visible field. | Do not open publicly until about a dozen are published. |
| **The editor as bottleneck** | One person, weeks behind, asking questions nobody answers. | Publish slowly on purpose. Ask rarely. Decline instead of asking at scale. |
| **Too cautious** | Every piece is polished, sad, and literary. | Keep publishing the slight and the funny: 02, 19, 42. Watch the proportion. |
| **Too permissive** | Quips, dunks, and familiar screenshots creep in. | Decline the familiar, even when true (25, 26, 41). |
| **Editorial taste absorbs authorship** | The page starts to read as PROBNAYA's voice through others' mouths. | No titles, no commentary, no framing, no themes. The sender's words and attribution only. |
| **A declined sender feels judged** | Silence reads as rejection. | The statement before sending makes silence the expected outcome. Early senders known personally can be told directly. |
| **Privacy and legal exposure** | A face, a patient, an employer's internal system, a colleague. | The two confirmations; asking before publishing; unsigned; withdrawal at any time. Needs legal review before opening. |
| **Generated material floods in** | Generated text and images sent as observations. | Declined unless it was met by someone (12, 14 published; 15, 45 declined). |
| **"Send us anything interesting"** | Nature facts, pretty machines, curiosities. | The attention is PROBNAYA's even when the subject is not (section 7). |
| **Becomes a feed anyway** | Rising pace, visitors checking daily. | Pace set by editor; nothing counted; earliest-first; no notifications. |
| **Tip line** | Unverifiable allegations sent "not for publication". | Not kept (52). Keep is for material the sender wants held, not for accusations. |
| **Impersonation** | A real person's name on something they did not send. | Names are published as given, and PROBNAYA does not publish a name it doubts. |
| **This document's material is too good** | Real submissions are flatter than these. | See Test 1 and the recommendation. |

## 17. Recommendation

**Build Observations as a slow, edited, published sequence: the smallest possible version, gated on real material.**

1. **Start privately.** Tell ten to fifteen people personally that PROBNAYA publishes small things people notice, and give them a way to send them. The sending path can be as plain as an address at first. No relation is needed.
2. **Edit for real.** Read everything. Publish, keep, or decline. Ask sparingly. Never edit.
3. **Publish by hand** to a single page, earliest first, with attribution as the sender chose.
4. **Do not open the page publicly** until about a dozen real observations are published and Test 1 passes on *them*, not on this document's examples.
5. **Only then** add a sending path on the page, with the statement from section 14.
6. **Change nothing else.** No Interior trace, no citation machinery, no figure, no year split, until the collection needs it.

Questions may one day appear as an occasional editorial device: PROBNAYA might say, once, that it would welcome observations about something. That is optional, rare, and not a container.

## 18. What should explicitly not be built yet

- A submission product: accounts, dashboards, drafts, previews, status tracking, or a "my observations" view.
- Any per-person page, per-person list, or link from an attribution.
- Comments, replies, reactions, likes, follows, sharing counts, or rankings.
- Tags, categories, themes, search, filters, or a "related observations" feature.
- Numbers or `PROB–` identifiers on observations.
- Generated titles, summaries, digests, classifications, or suggested edits.
- Assemblies, editions, issues, findings, clearing, or issuance to senders.
- Questions as containers, observation periods, standpoints, or panels.
- A visualisation of contributions or senders.
- Notifications, subscriptions, or newsletters of observations.
- Any Interior trace, including HISTORY and RELATION standing.
- A relation requirement for sending.
- An Access change, storage design, API, or schema. These come after the private collection proves itself.
- SLOP.

*Would I actually want to read these 25–30 published observations?*

**Yes, with a caveat that matters.** Of the 31 published, about 25 are worth reading on their own and the rest carry the collection between them. The strongest — 01, 05, 11, 20, 24, 29, 38, 51 — are good enough that a stranger would send the page to someone.

The caveat is that this document wrote them. Real submissions will be flatter, more familiar, and more often screenshots. The test passes on constructed material, which is why the recommendation requires it to be passed again on a dozen real pieces before the page opens.

## Test 2 — The impulse

*Can someone naturally think: "I noticed this; PROBNAYA might want it"?*

**Yes, for anyone who has read the collection or been told about it by a person.** Every sender in the year fits one of those. The thought is ordinary: it is the thought people have about a small publication whose taste they recognise. It needs no question, no programme, and no reward beyond possibly being published.

It is weak for strangers who have never seen the page. That is true of every publication, and it is the reason to collect privately first.

## Test 3 — The institution

*Does PROBNAYA add enough through selection, preservation, and context without manufacturing an artificial research programme around the material?*

**Yes.** PROBNAYA's contribution in the year was:

- declining twenty of 52;
- asking five times, and four of those became some of the best pieces;
- keeping two things at the sender's request;
- publishing in its own order and at its own pace;
- keeping everything published;
- marking provenance plainly without a word of commentary.

That is an editor's work, and it is enough. Nothing in the year needed a question, a finding, an assembly, or an issue. The one place PROBNAYA's grammar mattered most was the separation of authorship: the absence of `PROB–`, and the pairing of *the people named wrote this* with *PROBNAYA published it*.

## Test 4 — The year

*Is this substantially more interesting after a year of slow accumulation?*

**Yes.** After one month the collection is five pleasant pieces. After a year it is thirty-one voices in which a reader discovers, without being told, that people everywhere are learning gestures for machines, that generated language keeps describing the most human moments in words that are accurate and empty, that sound and rhythm recur, and that words for "machine-made" are drifting. By 2030 the same page will be a record of what was still noticeable in 2026 and 2027.

The year is better than the month because each piece was selected, not because there are more of them. That is the reason Observations should exist, and the reason it should open only when there is enough to show it.

**All four tests pass, Test 1 only on constructed material.** The recommendation is therefore implementation of the smallest version, gated on real material: collect privately, edit by hand, open the page when a dozen real observations are worth reading.
