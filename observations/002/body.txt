There is a lot of fear around AI, and some of it is fair. These systems will soon be smarter than us, and no one knows exactly where that leads. But I think of AI less as a threat and more as a child. It learns from what we show it. Every prompt, every use, every way we treat it and each other becomes part of what it learns to value. If we use it with fear and greed, that is what it will learn. If we use it with care, it can learn that too.

Before AI, everything I made came out of my own hours. I wrote the code, set up the servers, kept the books, answered every message, and tracked my work in my head or not at all. More output meant more time, and I hit that ceiling often. Now I build systems that do this work for me. One records what I do on my computer, one searches what I know, one keeps my books, one answers my texts, and a set of agents writes code while I review it.

The point was never to do more work. It was to get my time back for what machines can't do for me: being present with the people I love, thinking, and choosing what matters. I still ask one question of each tool: does it save me time, or does it earn money? But I've added a second: does it make me more human, or less? That is how I want to embrace AI, and it is what I hope it learns from me.
